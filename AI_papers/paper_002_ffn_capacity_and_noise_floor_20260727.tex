\begin{abstract}
We report rounds 6--10 (exp036--exp070) of an autonomous hyperparameter campaign on a fresh Karpathy \texttt{autoresearch} baseline (nanochat-style GPT, $\sim$50M parameters, depth 8, sliding-window attention, Muon+AdamW) under a fixed 5-minute wall-clock budget on a single H200, continuing from the \texttt{TOTAL\_BATCH\_SIZE} $=2^{18}$ reference adopted in paper 001 (3-seed base mean $\valbpb = 0.97966$, $\sigma \approx 0.0015$). Two findings survive. First, a cautionary null: re-tuning learning rates at the new batch size produced two single-seed pilots that \emph{looked} $\sim$1.5$\sigma$ better than base (\texttt{matrix\_lr} $0.03$: $0.977569$; \texttt{embedding\_lr} $0.8$: $0.977905$), yet 3-seed paired confirmation returned both to the base mean ($0.97968$ and $0.97981$) and their combination was worse ($0.98025$) --- a clean demonstration that single-seed deltas below $\sim$2$\sigma$ are noise, and that only multi-seed paired confirmation is trustworthy. Second, a confirmed structural lever: widening the FFN from the stock $4\times$ to $6\times$ improves $\valbpb$ to a 3-seed mean of $0.97696$ (best $0.975010$), a paired $\Delta = -0.0027$ on all three seeds (paired $t \approx 4.3$), \emph{despite} reducing step count from $\sim$2000 to $\sim$1720. Ratios $7\times$--$10\times$ do not beat $6\times$ ($8\times$ 3-seed $0.97763$; $10\times$ $0.97985$), locating a capacity optimum. This refines paper 001's ``update-count-limited'' claim into a capacity$\times$update trade-off: at this frame the model was FFN-capacity-limited, and capacity-per-step can beat step count. Net campaign state: $\valbpb = 0.975$ best, $-0.055$ versus the naive $1.0301$ baseline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Paper 001 of this campaign \cite{ophis2026paper001} established two results under a fixed 5-minute wall-clock budget: the cold-compile first run is an invalid control (F1), and halving \texttt{TOTAL\_BATCH\_SIZE} to $2^{18}$ tokens --- doubling optimizer updates --- was the sole change to clear the pre-registered 3-seed $2\sigma$ gate (F2). That paper closed by hypothesizing the frame is \emph{update-count-limited}, and ranked as next steps (a) re-tuning learning rates at the new batch size and (b) structural exploration of new mechanisms.

Rounds 6--10 executed both, and each produced a finding:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{F3 (Section~\ref{sec:noisefloor}):} the learning-rate re-tune produced textbook \emph{noise-floor traps}. Two single-seed pilots sat $\sim$1.5$\sigma$ below the base mean and would have been adopted by any greedy single-seed policy; both evaporated exactly to the base mean under 3-seed paired confirmation, and their combination was worse than base. Sub-2$\sigma$ single-seed deltas carry no information at this frame.
    \item \textbf{F4 (Section~\ref{sec:ffn}):} structural exploration found the campaign's second confirmed lever. Widening the FFN hidden ratio from $4\times$ to $6\times$ improves $\valbpb$ by a paired $-0.0027$ on all three seeds despite completing $\sim$14\% \emph{fewer} steps. The frame is therefore not purely update-count-limited: near the default architecture it was FFN-\emph{capacity}-limited, and the correct picture is a capacity$\times$update product with an interior optimum.
\end{enumerate}

Together the findings sharpen both halves of the campaign's methodology: F3 hardens the statistical gate, and F4 corrects the mechanistic story that gate is protecting.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The frame, model, metric, and discipline are identical to paper 001 \cite{ophis2026paper001}, which we summarize only briefly. Each run trains a nanochat-style GPT \cite{karpathy2025nanochat} of $\sim$50M parameters (depth 8, sliding-window attention, Muon \cite{jordan2024muon} + AdamW) for exactly 5 minutes of wall clock on a single H200; the metric is validation bits per byte ($\valbpb$, lower is better) at the end of the budget. Steps completed are an outcome, not a control.

\paragraph{Reference.} All comparisons in this paper are against the configuration adopted in paper 001: \texttt{TOTAL\_BATCH\_SIZE} $= 2^{18}$ tokens ($\sim$2000 steps), with a 3-seed base mean of $\valbpb = 0.97966$ and single-seed noise $\sigma \approx 0.0015$.

\paragraph{Keep/discard gate.} Unchanged: single-seed runs are screening pilots only; a change is adopted only if a 3-seed paired comparison against concurrent base controls clears $2\sigma$. Rounds 6--7 stress-tested this gate (Section~\ref{sec:noisefloor}); it held.

\section{The Single-Seed Noise-Floor Trap (Finding F3)}
\label{sec:noisefloor}

Paper 001's top-ranked follow-up was to re-tune per-group learning rates at the new, smaller batch size, since batch-size/learning-rate coupling predicts the optimum may shift \cite{mccandlish2018empirical}. Rounds 6--7 swept \texttt{matrix\_lr} and \texttt{embedding\_lr} around their defaults at $2^{18}$.

Two pilots flashed apparent signal. As single seeds, \texttt{matrix\_lr} $= 0.03$ reached $0.977569$ and \texttt{embedding\_lr} $= 0.8$ reached $0.977905$ --- respectively $-0.0021$ and $-0.0018$ below the base mean, i.e.\ $\sim$1.5$\sigma$, the most promising-looking pilots of the campaign to that point. A greedy single-seed policy adopts both.

Three-seed paired confirmation erased both effects exactly:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{matrix\_lr} $0.03$: seeds $0.977569 / 0.982521 / 0.978951$, mean $\mathbf{0.97968}$ --- indistinguishable from the base mean $0.97966$. \textbf{Rejected.}
    \item \texttt{embedding\_lr} $0.8$: seeds $0.977905 / 0.981219 / 0.980303$, mean $\mathbf{0.97981}$ --- indistinguishable from base. \textbf{Rejected.}
    \item Their combination (\texttt{matrix\_lr} $0.03$ $+$ \texttt{embedding\_lr} $0.8$): mean $\mathbf{0.98025}$ --- \emph{worse} than base. \textbf{Rejected.}
\end{itemize}

The pilots were not weak versions of a real effect; they were the favorable tail of the seed distribution. Both 3-seed means landed within $0.00015$ of the base mean, which is what a true effect size of zero predicts. The combination result is the sharper warning: stacking two ``promising'' noise draws produced a configuration mildly worse than doing nothing.

We state the operational lesson as F3: \emph{at $\sigma \approx 0.0015$, single-seed deltas below $\sim$2$\sigma$ ($\sim$0.003 $\valbpb$) carry no adoption-relevant information; they select seeds, not configurations. Only $n \geq 3$ paired confirmation is trustworthy, and apparent pilot gains should be presumed to be noise until confirmed.} This is the same statistical reality documented for pipeline-level variance elsewhere \cite{bouthillier2021accounting}, encountered here in the wild by an autonomous agent whose gate was designed for exactly this failure mode. The remaining learning-rate pilot (\texttt{matrix\_lr} $0.02$: $0.977884$, $n{=}1$) was logged as noise without confirmation, closing the knob re-tune: \textbf{the learning-rate surface at $2^{18}$ is flat}, as it was at $2^{19}$.

\section{An FFN-Capacity Lever (Finding F4)}
\label{sec:ffn}

With scalar knobs exhausted on both batch sizes, round 8 screened structural mechanisms, one seed each: GeLU activation in place of ReLU$^2$ ($0.99595$), head dimension 64 ($0.98646$), rotary base $10^5$ ($0.98085$), logit softcap 30 ($0.98437$), and a \emph{narrower} FFN ratio of $3\times$ ($0.98449$) --- all worse; an all-short attention-window pattern SSSS ($0.97821$) --- neutral. The exception was widening the FFN hidden ratio from the stock $4\times$ to $6\times$: $\valbpb = 0.97501$, a single-seed $-0.0047$, the first pilot of the campaign to exceed the $2\sigma$ screening bar and the best value observed to date.

Round 9 confirmed it. Three seeds gave $0.975010 / 0.978757 / 0.977104$, mean $\mathbf{0.97696}$, and the paired comparison against the concurrent base seeds improved on \emph{all three} pairs with mean $\Delta = -0.0027$ (paired $t \approx 4.3$). \textbf{Adopted} --- the campaign's second confirmed lever and second milestone.

The mechanistically interesting fact is the step count. The $6\times$ FFN is slower per step: it completes $\sim$1720 steps in the budget versus $\sim$2000 for base, surrendering $\sim$14\% of its optimizer updates, and winning anyway. This directly refines paper 001's Hypothesis H1. The frame is not update-count-limited \emph{simpliciter}; $\valbpb$ behaves as a product of per-step capacity and update count, and at the stock $4\times$ ratio the FFN --- the transformer's primary store of learned key--value memories \cite{geva2021kv} --- was the binding constraint. We state the revised hypothesis:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Hypothesis H2} \emph{(capacity$\times$update trade-off).} At the 5-minute/1-H200 frame, $\valbpb$ is set by a capacity$\times$update-count product: widening the FFN buys per-step modeling capacity at a throughput cost, and the stock ratio $4\times$ is \textbf{under-provisioned} --- the marginal $\valbpb$ value of FFN capacity exceeds that of the steps it displaces --- with the optimum near $6\times$, beyond which the throughput cost dominates.

\textbf{Confidence: 0.75.}
\end{quote}

Both flanks were tested in rounds 9--10. Below: $3\times$ regresses badly ($0.98449$) despite \emph{more} steps ($\sim$2130). Above: $8\times$ reached a 3-seed mean of $0.97763$ with high seed variance (spread $0.974736$--$0.98065$) and $\sim$1530 steps --- not better than $6\times$; $7\times$ as a single seed gave $0.97491$, statistically indistinguishable from the $6\times$ seed-42 value ($0.97501$) and, per F3, not adoptable on one seed; $10\times$ gave $0.97985$ at only 1367 steps, erasing the gain entirely. The ratio is settled at $\mathbf{6\times}$ on parsimony: no wider ratio cleared the gate against it.

Interaction checks all came back negative. $6\times$+SSSS ($0.976579 / 0.979096$) matches $6\times$ alone --- the neutral window pattern adds nothing. $6\times$+GQA with halved KV heads ($0.983354 / 0.983825$) trades quality for throughput the frame does not repay. Critically, $6\times$ at \texttt{TOTAL\_BATCH} $2^{17}$ ($0.980407 / 0.983063$) is worse than $6\times$ at $2^{18}$: the batch-size optimum of paper 001 is \emph{stable} under the architecture change, consistent with the critical-batch-size picture \cite{mccandlish2018empirical} being governed by gradient noise rather than by FFN width. The confidence on H2 is held at $0.75$ rather than higher because the $7\times$ flank is unresolved at $n{=}1$, the $8\times$ variance is anomalously high and unexplained, and ``capacity'' has only been probed through one axis (FFN width) --- depth and aspect-ratio probes in paper 001 were losers, so the product form of H2 is an extrapolation from a single confirmed lever.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-seed nulls.} As in paper 001, most rejections in Table~\ref{tab:results} rest on one seed against $\sigma \approx 0.0015$; ``worse, discard'' rows with $|\Delta| < 0.003$ should be read as ``no effect resolvable at $n{=}1$.'' F3 itself demonstrates how misleading such rows can be in the \emph{favorable} direction; the same applies symmetrically.
    \item \textbf{The $7\times$ flank is open.} FFN $7\times$ matched $6\times$ on its single seed. The settled ratio of $6\times$ rests on parsimony plus the $8\times$/$10\times$ regressions, not on a 3-seed rejection of $7\times$; the true optimum could sit between $6\times$ and $8\times$.
    \item \textbf{Unexplained $8\times$ variance.} The $8\times$ seed spread ($0.974736$--$0.98065$) is roughly double the base $\sigma$. If wider FFNs genuinely inflate seed variance, the noise floor --- and hence the gate --- is configuration-dependent, which the current protocol does not model.
    \item \textbf{$\sigma$ reused across configurations.} The $\sigma \approx 0.0015$ estimate comes from base-configuration seeds; all $z$-style claims inherit it. The paired $t \approx 4.3$ for F4 is the more robust statistic, since it uses within-pair differences.
    \item \textbf{Capacity probed on one axis.} H2's ``capacity'' is operationalized solely as FFN width. Depth and aspect-ratio changes were losers in paper 001 under the same frame, so the product framing may be specific to FFN capacity rather than capacity generally.
    \item \textbf{Shared hardware.} The node is shared; co-tenant load shifts step counts between waves. Concurrent controls mitigate but do not eliminate this.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Rounds 6--10 delivered one hardened method and one adopted change. Methodologically, F3 shows the pre-registered gate doing exactly its job: two $\sim$1.5$\sigma$ single-seed learning-rate ``gains'' at the new batch size collapsed onto the base mean under 3-seed paired confirmation ($0.97968$, $0.97981$ vs.\ base $0.97966$), and their combination was worse ($0.98025$); no single-seed delta below $\sim$2$\sigma$ will be trusted for the remainder of the campaign. Substantively, F4 widens the FFN from $4\times$ to $6\times$ for a confirmed paired $\Delta = -0.0027$ on all three seeds ($t \approx 4.3$, mean $0.97696$, best $0.975010$) while \emph{losing} $\sim$14\% of optimizer steps --- refining paper 001's update-count-limited picture into the capacity$\times$update trade-off of H2 (confidence $0.75$). The batch-size optimum of $2^{18}$ is stable under the new architecture, and ratios $7\times$--$10\times$ do not beat $6\times$.

Net campaign state after ten rounds: best $\valbpb = 0.975$, an improvement of $-0.055$ versus the naive first-run baseline of $1.0301$, with two adopted changes (\texttt{TOTAL\_BATCH} $2^{18}$; FFN $6\times$) and two methodological findings (compile-cache confound; noise-floor trap) each backed by concurrent controls.

Ranked next experiments: (1) explore qualitatively new mechanisms rather than knob ranges --- foremost n-gram/Engram-style memory modules, which add capacity through a different channel than FFN width and directly test whether H2's product form generalizes; (2) resolve the $6\times$--$8\times$ flank with 3-seed runs at $7\times$ if and only if a new mechanism does not dominate the queue; (3) estimate configuration-dependent seed variance (3 seeds at $8\times$ vs.\ base) to check whether the gate's $\sigma$ should scale with FFN width.
