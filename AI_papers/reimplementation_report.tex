\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This campaign searched for architectural and optimisation improvements to a byte-level
language model under a fixed \SI{300}{\second} wall-clock budget on a single H200, scoring
held-out \bpb{} on a pinned validation shard. Over roughly three weeks and 388 recorded runs
it closed \textbf{28 directions}, produced \textbf{one adoption}, and \textbf{retracted that
adoption one block later} when it was verified on seeds disjoint from those used to select
it. The retraction is the campaign's principal result: the effect proved real
($-0.002139$ pooled over 20 pairs, $t=-7.18$, 20/20 seeds favouring treatment) but
\emph{sub-gate}, and the margin that had carried it over the adoption floor is best
explained as selection bias. The campaign's standing state is \textbf{no adopted result},
and its registered scope baseline is flagged \textbf{contaminated} after a session in which
45 of 48 runs completed $\sim$1147 optimizer steps against an expected $\sim$2000 under GPU
contention. We report the ledger, the retraction in full, a provenance grading of every
headline figure, and the conditions under which comparison with sibling campaigns is and is
not meaningful.
\end{abstract}

\section{The contaminated baseline}
\label{sec:contamination}

The registered scope baseline of \num{0.934930} ($n=10$) was measured during a session in
which \textbf{45 of 48 runs completed $\sim$1147 optimizer steps against an expected
$\sim$2000} for the same \SI{300}{\second} budget. The cause was GPU contention from another
tenant.

The measured penalty was $+\num{0.005390}$ relative to the same configuration measured
clean. Note the magnitude: contention cost roughly \textbf{twice} the campaign's entire
adoption gate. In a wall-clock frame, a co-tenant does not add noise --- it removes
optimizer steps, and the metric follows.

The baseline was downgraded to \texttt{pending} in \texttt{reconciliation.json} v32 and
\texttt{authorize-run} is correctly blocked in this scope until a genuine
$\geq\!10$-seed remeasurement replaces it.

A second instance appears in the 2026-07-30 reproduction: seeds 42 and 43 were
\textbf{quarantined} after another tenant saturated their GPUs mid-run (step time
$150 \rightarrow \SI{336}{\milli\second}$, MFU $19\% \rightarrow 8.6\%$, $\sim$956 of
$\sim$2000 steps). They scored \num{0.989} and \num{0.995} and were discarded rather than
averaged in. Had they been included, the reproduction's mean would have been corrupted by
approximately \num{0.012} --- five gates --- by two runs out of ten.

\section{The adoption, and its retraction}

\subsection{E28: the campaign's first adoption}

\texttt{ASPECT\_RATIO}) with \texttt{MATRIX\_LR=0.03}, against frozen defaults
\texttt{DEPTH=8} (dim 768) and \texttt{MATRIX\_LR=0.04}.

The record itself flags three reservations at the time of adoption, before any verification
was run:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The margin is thin.} The point estimate exceeds the floor by \SI{4}{\percent},
        and the upper end of the 95\% CI ($-0.001669$) lies \emph{below} the floor in
        magnitude, so the true effect may not clear.
  \item \textbf{Against the registered baseline} (\num{0.931857}) rather than the in-tranche
        control, the delta is $-0.002349 = 0.90$ gates, which does \emph{not} clear. Both
        numbers were recorded so the discrepancy was visible rather than buried.
  \item \textbf{The prediction was that it would fail.} The launcher recorded an expected
        $\sim$0.88 gates. The result came in \num{0.000415} above the sum of its parts
        ($-0.002002$ for depth 6 at $n=12$; $-0.000298$ for the LR at $n=4$), and that
        super-additivity was explicitly logged as \emph{measured, not explained}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Diagnosis: winner's curse, predicted in advance}

The configuration was \emph{selected} from sweeps run on seeds 42--45 (depth 6 from E10;
\texttt{MATRIX\_LR=0.03} from E26/E27) and then \emph{evaluated} on seeds 42--51, which
contain all four selection seeds. Selection and evaluation on overlapping draws inflates the
estimate.

This was named in the reading log \textbf{one block before the verification ran}, with a
predicted regression toward the additive estimate of $\sim$0.88 gates. The measured
regression was $+\num{0.001153}$, from 1.04 gates to 0.60 --- close to the prediction.

The \num{0.000415} super-additive residual that carried E28 over the floor, recorded at the
time as \emph{measured, not explained}, is now best explained as selection bias.

\subsection{What survives}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The effect is real and it is sub-gate.} Pooled over 20 pairs:
$-\num{0.002139}$, $t=-7.18$, 20/20 seeds better, 95\% CI
$[-\num{0.002762},\,-\num{0.001515}]$ --- excludes zero, does not reach the floor.
\end{quote}

Depth 6 with \texttt{MATRIX\_LR=0.03} genuinely beats the frozen defaults by about
\num{0.0021} \bpb{}; the gate asks for \num{0.002614}. The snapshot is retained as the
best-known configuration with \texttt{repro.json} marked retracted. \textbf{It is not a
SOTA.}

\section{Comparison with sibling campaigns}

Two sibling campaigns optimise the same objective on the same host against the same
validation shard. Comparison is meaningful only where the model and the batch size match.

\caption{The two campaigns at dim 512 / $2^{19}$ are directly comparable to each other.
Neither is directly comparable to this one.}
\label{tab:siblings}

Two observations follow, and the second is the more useful.

\paragraph{Step counts are not a unit of work.} This campaign runs $\sim$2000 optimizer
steps per \SI{300}{\second} frame against $\sim$975 for the dim-512 campaigns, which invites
the reading that it is twice as fast. At $2^{18}$ versus $2^{19}$ tokens per step, the
throughputs are \num{524} million and \num{511} million tokens respectively ---
within \SI{3}{\percent}. The step-count difference is entirely batch size.

\paragraph{The width--batch interaction is unexplored on both sides.} This campaign operates
at dim 768 with batch $2^{18}$. Campaign \texttt{v1} tested dim 768 \emph{at batch $2^{19}$}
and measured $+\num{0.078976}$ --- one of its largest negatives --- while never varying
\texttt{TOTAL\_BATCH\_SIZE} in 189 experiments. Those two facts are jointly consistent only
if width and batch size interact strongly. Neither campaign has probed that diagonal.

\section{Discussion}

Three properties of this campaign's record are worth stating plainly, because they are
unusual and they are what make the negative result credible.

\paragraph{The verification was run against interest.} E29 was executed on disjoint seeds
immediately after an adoption the campaign had every incentive to keep --- its first in 28
directions. The retraction was published one block later.

\paragraph{The failure mode was predicted before it was measured.} The reading log named the
overlap between selection and evaluation seeds, and estimated the regression, before E29
ran. The measured regression matched. A campaign that can predict its own errors in advance
is calibrated in a way that a campaign reporting only successes cannot demonstrate.

\paragraph{Contamination is treated as invalidity, not as noise.} Both the rejected baseline
and the two quarantined seeds were removed rather than averaged. In a wall-clock frame this
is not fastidiousness: contention removed roughly half the optimizer steps in the affected
runs and cost \num{0.005390} \bpb{}, about twice the adoption gate. Averaging contaminated
runs into a mean does not add variance --- it moves the mean.

\paragraph{The cost.} The same discipline produces one adoption in three weeks, and then
withdraws it. The campaign's headline \SI{10}{\percent} improvement is historical, earned
early from a handful of large mechanisms, and its recent record is a plateau punctuated by a
retraction. Whether that is a well-calibrated search or an over-tight gate is not settled by
the record: a floor of \num{0.002614} rejects a real, 20/20-consistent effect of
\num{0.002139}, and no analysis in the campaign asks whether the floor is correctly placed.
