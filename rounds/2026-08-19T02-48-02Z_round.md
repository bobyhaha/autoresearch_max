## explorer (explorer-r1)

**Framing.** L005 changes what is worth buying. At `final_epoch 2.0` the marginal step re-reads tokens the model has already seen, so throughput levers now buy repetition, not information. The family that survives that is per-step update *quality* — which is exactly `optimizer_numeric`. All five proposals below act through the update rule and none change step count materially; each is expected to act in either regime, and each must read `final_epoch` off its own wave's control.

Calibration used throughout: at ~4.2 bytes/token, `Δbpb ≈ Δnats / 2.9`. The 0.00069 band is therefore ~0.002 nats — every proposal below is priced against that, not against a hand-wave.

**Code fact that drives three of these.** With `DEPTH=8`, `n_embd=512`, `HEAD_DIM=128`, the Muon groups are exactly three shapes: `(512,512)`, `(2048,512)` (`mlp.c_fc`), `(512,2048)` (`mlp.c_proj`). The LR factor at train.py:415 is `max(1.0, shape[-2]/shape[-1])**0.5` → `1.0, 2.0, 1.0`. An orthogonalized update has RMS `1/sqrt(max(m,n))`, so realized update RMS is `0.0442, 0.0442, 0.0221`. **The MLP down-projection receives exactly half the update RMS of every other matrix in the model**, and it is `zeros_`-initialized (train.py:170), so it is the parameter furthest from equilibrium at 1000 steps. The tall branch equalizes RMS; the wide branch is simply uncovered.

## pragmatist (pragmatist-r1)

### The price list I am ranking against

Everything below is priced against two numbers I recomputed from `runs/sweep/results/*.json` rather than assumed.

**Steps→bpb slope.** The four sequential controls (621→1.025517, 701→1.016505, 729→1.013602, 815→1.005527) fit −0.0735 bpb per e-fold of steps with almost no residual (the 701→729 pair alone gives −0.074). The twelve concurrent controls give −0.052 bpb/e-fold across the 1010 vs 1019 split. Use **−0.055 bpb/e-fold** in the current 2-epoch regime.

**Break-even throughput cost.** Noise band 0.00069 ÷ 0.055 = **1.25% of step time**. Median step is 297.5 ms (`step_ms_med`), so a mechanism may spend **~3.7 ms/step** before its throughput cost alone eats the band. That is the budget. It is much larger than this campaign's prose implies.

**The 300 s is fully charged.** `train.py:551,580` brackets every step in `torch.cuda.synchronize()`; `dt` therefore includes optimizer GPU time. There is no hiding place.

**What the optimizer actually costs.** Muon matrices are 8×(4× 512² + 2× 2048×512) = 25.2 M params. Newton–Schulz per iteration is 4mn²+2n³ ⇒ 64.4 GFLOP, ×5 = **322 GFLOP/step**, against 125 TFLOP/step of fwd+bwd (from mfu 42.59% × 989.5e12 × 0.2975). That is 0.26% of the FLOPs, ~1.3 ms wall at a realistic 250 TFLOP/s for batched 512-ish GEMMs — **0.4% of the step**. The whole orthogonalization is a third of the noise band.

### precond="pre" — RANK 1

**BUILDABLE: yes, already, and I verified the generated source.** `make_variant.py:274-311` deletes the NorMuon block verbatim and reinserts it verbatim after `g = stacked_grads.lerp_(momentum_buffer, momentum)`. I generated it: the block lands intact, `fullgraph=True` survives, no new tensor, no new op, no branch, and the `stacked_params.sub_(lr*g + lr*wd*stacked_params*mask)` line — the exact fused bf16→fp32→bf16 update that `b1_optimizer_fusion_break_costs_loss` is about — is untouched. **Zero fusion-break risk.**

**THROUGHPUT COST: structurally zero, not estimated-zero.** `g` has identical shape in both positions (the momentum and the orthogonalized update are both `[num, m, n]`), `red_dim` is unchanged, `second_momentum_buffer` shape is unchanged. Same op count, same tensor sizes. The only way this costs time is a different Inductor fusion decision, which the cuda-event timer below would catch.

**The property that makes it testable at n=1:** the polar iteration opens with `X = X / (X.norm()*1.02 + 1e-6)`, so any *global* rescale the NorMuon block applies to the momentum is discarded outright; only the per-row/column *shape* survives. And the update magnitude is identical either way — baseline emits `‖NS output‖_F` (NorMuon's `v_norm/v_norm_new` is norm-preserving by construction), pre-position emits `‖NS output‖_F` directly. **This reorder is LR-neutral.** That matters enormously: `b0_muon2_second_moment_before_ns` swept LR separately for each method, and we cannot afford a sweep. Here we do not need one.

Numerics are safe: only `wte` and `value_embeds` are bf16 (`train.py:186,188`); Muon matrices are fp32, so `second_momentum_buffer` is fp32 and the smaller pre-NS magnitudes carry no precision hazard.

**IMPLEMENTATION RISK: lowest of any candidate.** The activation observable (`precond_secmom_rms`) is computed outside the compiled function and reads a statistic that must move by orders of magnitude (raw-momentum scale vs the ~1/√512 ≈ 0.044 of an orthogonalized matrix). It cannot silently no-op the way `ema` did.

Evidence weight: three independent sources say pre beats post, and `b0_muon2_beats_output_side_adaptive_variants` names **NorMuon specifically** — the thing the baseline runs — as the loser. Reported effects (−1.56 ppl GPT-Small; −0.38 ppl ≈ 0.015 nats LLaMA-60M) are 20×+ the band.

### wd_const — RANK 2, but only as a two-arm bracket

**BUILDABLE: yes**, `make_variant.py:341` replaces `get_weight_decay`'s body. **THROUGHPUT COST: exactly zero** — it changes a value written into a 0-D CPU tensor already being written every step.

Baseline is `0.2*(1-progress)`, integrated mean 0.1, applied only to Muon matrices and *cautiously* (the `(g*p)>=0` mask gates roughly half the elements, so effective decay is ~0.5× nominal). The evidence is genuinely split: `o0_large_weight_decay_for_short_horizons` says 0.5 beats 0.1 at short horizons, while `b0_moonlight_weight_decay_helps_only_when_overtrained` says no-WD converges faster early and WD only wins in the overtrained regime. We are at 534 M tokens over a ~267 M-token dataset (`final_epoch 2.0`) — undertrained on *unique* data but seeing every token twice, which is exactly where those two claims point opposite ways.

**Therefore do not run a single guessed value.** Run `wd_const=0.0` and `wd_const=0.3` in one wave with the control. A single arm resolves nothing; the bracket resolves the sign at zero throughput cost. Literature WD sweeps move loss 0.005–0.02, well above band.

### Optimizer cuda-event timer — a rider, not a slot

`make_variant.py:55` declares `_t_opt=0.0` and **never writes it.** We have `loader_frac` and `fwdbwd_frac` and no measurement whatsoever of optimizer cost. Two `sub()` calls fix this: record `torch.cuda.Event` pairs around `optimizer.step()`, append them, and query `elapsed_time` after the loop (never inside it). Cost is two event records per step, microseconds, and no added sync because the step is already synchronize-bracketed. Ride it on the precond wave. It converts every future optimizer proposal from arithmetic to measurement — including the one below, whose entire verdict currently hinges on an ambiguity I cannot resolve on paper.

### MuonEq (row/column equilibration) — RANK 3, gated

**BUILDABLE: yes, but it is the only candidate requiring new code.** Scale rows/cols of the momentum to unit norm, orthogonalize, unscale. New elementwise reductions inside `muon_step_fused` — torch.compile handles those fine, no graph break, no data-dependent branch, and the fused final update line stays untouched. The real cost is the **observable**: the equilibration factors are transient inside the compiled graph, so proving engagement needs a new persistent buffer and a new argument to `muon_step_fused`. Unobservable mechanisms are precisely the failure class `VariantEditError` exists to prevent.

**THROUGHPUT: I cannot price the claimed "+4%".** If it means 4% of the *optimizer*, that is 0.05% of our step — free. If it means 4% of the *step*, that is 11.9 ms ⇒ 0.0022 bpb, **3× the band**, and it must clear that before it wins anything. The cuda-event timer resolves this; nothing else does.

**Gate it.** MuonEq and precond are the *same intervention family* — both reshape row/column scales before orthogonalization; precond via an EMA second moment (zero new code), MuonEq via instantaneous norms (new code, new buffer, new observable). precond strictly dominates on implementation risk. Run MuonEq only if precond flashes above the band.

### KILLS

**clip — kill.** Muon's update is scale-invariant: `X = X/(‖X‖·1.02+1e-6)` discards any global gradient rescale, and NorMuon's `v_norm/v_norm_new` is scale-invariant too. Adam with `eps=1e-10` is scale-invariant to a *constant* factor. So a global clip is a no-op except through the varying per-step factor's effect on momentum-buffer weighting — a spike suppressor. With a stable loss curve over ~1000 steps, that is far below band. Worse: we have **no gradient-norm telemetry**, so we cannot choose a threshold that binds. A non-binding threshold is a byte-different variant that is mathematically identical to control — a burned slot that also contaminates the band with a fake treatment.

**ns — kill, and flag the trap.** `polar_express_coeffs` has exactly **5 entries** and the code slices `[:ns_steps]`. **`ns=6` or higher produces a source-different, hash-different, mathematically IDENTICAL variant, and `sub()` raises nothing.** Only ns ∈ {1,2,3,4} are real, and every registered claim (`b2_muon_newton_schulz_five_iterations_is_enough`, `o0_muon_ns_steps_five_is_enough`, `b0_moonlight_ns5_enough`) says 5 is optimal and lower degrades. The throughput argument is dead too: 5→3 saves 0.17% of the step ≈ **0.00009 bpb**, one-eighth of the band. Even the `b0_muon2_enables_ns3` combination arm is not worth a slot — the saving it monetizes does not exist at our operating point.

**NAMO — kill.** The NS output's Frobenius norm is ≈√min(m,n) by construction with very low step-to-step variance, so an Adam-style scalar on it converges to a near-constant. NAMO degenerates into a fixed `MATRIX_LR` multiplier wearing a mechanism's clothes — an LR change we neither chose nor can read off. Also note `_step_muon` already applies `max(1.0, m/n)**0.5` shape-aware LR, so `b0_moonlight_shape_aware_update_rms` (−0.023) is **already banked**; nobody should propose it.

**Gap worth naming:** `MATRIX_LR` is not an axis in `direction.py`. If the council wants the effect NAMO gestures at, the honest version is a direct LR arm at zero throughput cost — not an adaptive scalar that hides the number.

2. **wd_const bracket (0.0 and 0.3)** — zero build, zero throughput, above-band effect, but sign genuinely unknown; the bracket is what makes it worth slots.

## critic (critic-r1)

I checked every proposal against all five active lessons (L001 co-tenancy, L002 burned-GPU reuse, L003 wave split, L004 dispatcher double-booking, L005 operating point moved) and against the 184 registered claims for `optimizer_numeric`, 22 of which carry `stance: opposes`.

**A dilution factor that applies to every proposal on this list.** Muon governs only the 2D matrices: depth 8 × dim 512 ≈ 25M matrix parameters out of the ~134M total implied by 534M tokens at the stated ~4 TPP. Token/value embeddings, lm_head and the scalars are on AdamW. Every source claim here (Muon2 at GPT-Small 124M, MuonEq at LLaMA2-130M, NorMuon, VAMuon) ran Muon over the bulk of the model. Scale any imported effect by roughly 0.2 before comparing it to the 0.00069 band. Also note `fwdbwd_frac = 0.059` and `loader_frac = 0.667`: GPU-side work is ~6% of wall clock, so GPU-cost arguments are noise, and step-time observables cannot resolve anything cheaper than a few percent of the *fwd/bwd*, i.e. nothing on this list.

**ns — kill upward, and there is a harness bug.** `polar_express_coeffs` is a list of exactly **5** tuples (train.py:304-310) and the loop is `for a,b,c in polar_express_coeffs[:ns_steps]`. `ns=6`, `ns=10`, `ns=20` all slice to the same 5 tuples. **Upward `ns` is a silent no-op**: it produces a byte-different variant with a different hash and numerically identical training. It would burn a GPU-hour and enter the band as a fake "engaged and did nothing", and no declared observable would catch it — step_ms cannot, since NS on stacked 512×512 matrices is invisible inside a 297ms step at 6% fwd/bwd share. This is doubly damning because `b0_moonlight_ns5_enough` and `b2_muon_newton_schulz_five_iterations_is_enough` (validity 3/4, T_NS ∈ {5,10,20} comparable, 5 slightly best) already predict null. Do not run ns>5; fix the slice or delete the option. Downward is different: `b0_muon2_enables_ns3` records plain Muon degrading 29.51→32.70 ppl at Ns=3 on GPT-Small — roughly 0.06 bpb, ~0.012 after dilution, ~17x the band. So `ns=3` is not a SOTA candidate, it is the **positive control this campaign does not have**: a known-sign, above-band effect that proves the yoked-pair instrument can detect anything at all. If ns=3 comes back inside the band, the instrument is broken and every null this round is uninterpretable. That is the only version of `ns` I will clear.

**clip — kill on construction.** `make_variant` emits `torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), c)` before `optimizer.step()`: a single global-norm rescale of all gradients by a scalar. The polar iteration's first line is `X = X / (X.norm(dim=(-2,-1),keepdim=True)*1.02 + 1e-6)` — per-matrix Frobenius normalization. **A uniform gradient rescale is exactly cancelled for every Muon-governed parameter.** The cautious-WD mask `(g*p)>=0` is sign-based and scale-invariant; the NorMuon block operates on the already-normalized `X`. The only surviving pathway is the Nesterov momentum EMA mixing differently-scaled steps across time — a second-order effect — plus the AdamW groups, where Adam's ratio is likewise scale-invariant to a per-step constant except through its own state history. Independently, `o1_adaptive_clipping_useless_at_stable_lr` (validity 3/4) measures 2.24 vs 2.27, 2.33 vs 2.34, 2.46 vs 2.46 in stable-LR regimes; 16 controls with no divergence say we are stable. Predicted effect: at or below 0.00069. **Not runnable at n=1, and not runnable at n=5 either.** There is no declared activation observable — nothing prints the pre-clip grad norm or a clip-hit rate — so "threshold never bound" is indistinguishable from "bound and did nothing". If anyone insists, the minimum entry price is a clip-hit fraction printed by both arms and a per-matrix (not global) clip, which is a different mechanism.

**wd_const — the strongest knob, wrongly framed.** The baseline is `WEIGHT_DECAY * (1 - progress)`, i.e. 0.2 decaying **linearly to zero**, so WD is off precisely during the warmdown (WARMDOWN_RATIO=0.5, FINAL_LR_FRAC=0.0). Every late-crossover claim — `b5_weight_decay_hurts_early_helps_after_decay`, `b0_moonlight_weight_decay_helps_only_when_overtrained`, `b5_hyperball_late_crossover_vs_weightdecay` — says WD's payoff arrives *after* the LR decays. Our run has a full decay to zero over half its wall clock, so that window exists; the baseline simply doesn't put WD in it. So `wd_const` is not "more weight decay", it is a two-factor change: higher mean **and** WD alive through warmdown. ### Adjudication 1 — `precond`: the critic wins the taxonomy, the pragmatist wins the run

I read the generated diff. The block that moves computes `v_mean = g.square().mean(dim=red_dim)`, EMAs it at β₂=0.95, and rescales `g` by `rsqrt` of that, with a norm-preserving renormalization. Reducing over **one** axis is a per-row (or per-column) RMS rescale — it is not Muon2's elementwise second moment of the raw gradient, and it is not Adafactor-factored either, because Adafactor is a row⊗col outer product and this is rank-1 along a single axis. **The explorer's "Muon2 / Muon2-F" attribution is wrong and must not enter the writeup or the belief registry.** The critic's reading — MuonEq-R with an EMA — is the correct one, and the effect prior should be anchored on the MuonEq number, not the Muon2 number.

The critic is also right that it is two factors: the move *removes* output-side adaptivity and *adds* input-side equilibration, so a win cannot be attributed to the addition. But this changes the writeup, not the queue, for one reason: **the two factors are confounded and aligned.** `b0_muon2_beats_output_side_adaptive_variants` predicts the removal helps; `o2_muoneq_rownorm_before_ns_beats_muon_at_130m` predicts the addition helps. Both point the same way. For the objective — lower `val_bpb` — attribution is irrelevant and adoption is everything. For the registry, attribution matters, and the entry must read "reorder of the NorMuon block, two factors, not separable".

On the pragmatist's LR-neutrality claim I checked the arithmetic and it **survives the critic's objection**: the control's `final_scale = step_size * (v_norm/v_norm_new)` is norm-preserving by construction, so the post-NS update has ‖·‖_F ≈ √min(m,n) in the control; with the block removed, the raw NS output has that same norm. In the treatment the pre-NS rescale is discarded outright by `X = X/(‖X‖·1.02+1e-6)`. Both arms deliver an identically-normed update to first order. So the critic's demanded ‖lr·g‖/‖p‖ guard is satisfied by construction; I am not spending a build on it.
