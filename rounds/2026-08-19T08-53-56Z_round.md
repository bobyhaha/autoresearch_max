corpus using `claims.diagnostic_would_discriminate` before writing it down here.

(heads=3, HEAD_DIM=128 divides evenly). Mechanism: trade width for depth at roughly matched
or slightly *lower* FLOPs (depth·dim² ≈ 1.77M vs control's 2.10M, ~84%), which both tests
whether depth generalizes better than width in a 1000-step/2-epoch regime and should buy
extra steps from the lower per-token FLOP cost — a double channel on val_bpb given the
measured -0.03987 bpb/e-fold step law. Falsifier: val_bpb does not beat the running best

lower nominal FLOPs (would indict a bad implementation, e.g. kernel-shape penalties at
dim=384). Diagnostic: `depth` field, rule `gt 10` — verified `ok: 62/62 controls FAIL` (control
is always depth=8). Predicted effect: plausibly >0.001 given this is the first FLOP-neutral
architectural-shape probe the campaign has run at all.

**2. Z-loss objective regularizer (family: objective, zero runs, IMPLEMENTED mechanism
`zloss`, never tried).** `{"zloss":1e-4}` adds PaLM-style `logsumexp²` penalty on the vocab
partition function. Mechanism: constrains logit-scale drift under only 1000 steps of an
aggressive Muon+AdamW schedule with a softcap already at 15 — a regularizer that keeps the
softmax numerically well-conditioned should reduce late-training calibration noise that a

treats as legitimate for a genuinely new observable). Falsifier: `zloss_frac_of_total` stays
below 0.001 (mechanism did not materially engage — inconclusive, not a null) or val_bpb
moves inside the noise band.

**3. Long-window layer pruning (family: attention, axis `win` untouched).**
`{"win":"SSSSSSSL"}` — only the mandatory final layer stays Long; the other 7 become Short,
versus control's 6S/2L. Mechanism: swdiv=4 is already a confirmed win in this campaign,

## pragmatist (pragmatist-4)

**(a) STACK confirmation → adopt, don't keep stacking.** `precond=pre` (-0.001147, t=-12.3), `ve=1` (-0.001487, t=-12.2) and `swdiv=4` (~-0.002 device-corrected) are each individually 4-7x the 0.000287 GPU-counterbalanced resolution — these are not marginal calls, they're established. A confirming R4X quad landing near -0.0026 (sub-additive vs the naive sum of -0.00477, which is the expected and unremarkable outcome of stacking three wins) is 9x resolution, so one quad is enough evidence; a second confirming stack-quad buys almost nothing more. Recommend: adopt `precond=pre, ve=1, swdiv=4` into `PLATFORM` in `direction.py` the moment R4X reads out near-additive. The real cost is not the 39-run corpus itself — the pooled within-GPU sd (0.000203, resolution 0.000287) is a property of the box and should transfer — the cost is the four **per-GPU mean offsets** (gpu4 0.993759 … gpu7 0.991243), which recenter around the new baseline mean and need re-measuring. That's one fresh wave of 4 concurrent new-baseline controls (~8 min, ~0.5 GPU-hr), not a corpus rebuild. Delaying adoption is the more expensive path: every subsequent axis run (15 UNEXPLORED axes are waiting) gets tested against an inferior baseline and may need redoing if there's an interaction with the stack later. Recenter once, then explore forward from the better base.

**(b) Sweep status via rung-to-rung, honestly.** `balance.py`'s own ladder currently shows exactly **one rung** for every axis — `ns`, `qk_suppress`, `swdiv`, `tbs`, `ve`, `wd_const` — because R4S8 (`swdiv=8`, rung 2) hasn't landed yet. `direction.py`'s "closed (dry)" status on `ve`/`tbs`/`qk_suppress` means repeated runs at the *same* value stopped beating the running best, not that a second rung was tried and flattened — that's replication exhaustion, not saturation. So strictly nothing is saturated by the campaign's own marginal test yet. `swdiv` is the one axis about to get a real answer (R4S8, already queued) — keep it, it's the only sweep with a second rung in flight. Do not queue `swdiv=16` before reading that result. Do not spend more quads re-testing `qk_suppress=0.1` or `ve=1`/`precond=pre` at their known values — those are EXPLOIT-bucket only if stacked into something new, otherwise they're REPLICATE and add nothing at t>12.

**(c) tbs=18 is worth a quad, and it's cheap to argue.** `tbs=20` doubled tokens/step (524288→1048576) and *halved* num_steps (1019→~507) while final_epoch stayed 2.0 both ways — confirming this box is loader-bound, not compute-bound, so total tokens processed in 300s is roughly invariant to `tbs`; only the update *frequency* changes. The observed damage from `tbs=20` (+0.0227) lines up almost exactly with the diagnostic step-law prediction for halving steps (ln(0.5)×-0.03987 ≈ +0.0276) — i.e. the harm is well explained by *fewer, coarser updates*, not by some unrelated capacity effect. Symmetry says `tbs=18` should roughly double steps (~2000) at the same token/epoch exposure and buy back a comparable-magnitude gain — an order of magnitude bigger than any single knob confirmed so far, for the same one-quad cost. Bonus: `tbs=18` is a clean boundary case — `tokens_per_fwdbwd = DEVICE_BATCH_SIZE(128) * MAX_SEQ_LEN(2048) = 2^18`, and `TOTAL_BATCH_SIZE % tokens_per_fwdbwd == 0` requires `tbs >= 18` at the current `dbs`. So `tbs=18` sets `grad_accum_steps=1` — the floor of this lever without also touching `dbs`, no compounding, no assertion risk. Queue it as a genuine quad (not a replicate) now; the diagnostic law is explicitly not a verdict, so this is a bet worth the ~20 min, not a foregone conclusion.

**(d) Not worth the wall clock.** Further `tbs=20` replicates (dead, t-huge already). Further `unet` runs (+0.004123, 14x resolution, unambiguously bad). Repeating `qk_suppress=0.1` or `noqknorm`-adjacent single-value reruns without a genuinely new rung. A fourth or fifth stack-confirmation quad once R4X reads out cleanly — recenter and move on instead. `tbs=17` or lower without also moving `dbs` — it's not reachable cleanly (assertion fails) and forcing it compounds two knobs into an uninterpretable result. And don't let capacity (`depth`/`dim`/`mlp`) jump the queue ahead of the 15 UNEXPLORED, zero/near-zero-FLOP axes (`win`, `rope`, `softcap`, `x0init`, `ema`, `warmup`, `mu_const`, `clip`, `compile_mode`) — at mfu ~42.6% the GPU is genuinely busy, so FLOP-buying capacity knobs cost steps for a return that's unproven, while the coverage-floor axes are flagged "free" cost and structurally must be run before any of them can be closed.

## critic (critic-4)

Checked against: L035 (ve density, single-seed, void gpu4 cell), L020 (device offset, GPU-counterbalanced resolution), L024 (precond significance correction, cross-GPU pairing inflates variance), L028 (all runs seed 42, precision-without-reproducibility), L029/L031 (control budget, stranded waves), L032/L040 (valid negatives that block keys), L039 (non_activation ≠ negative), L019 (GPU is not idle, added FLOPs cost steps), L033 (component share ≠ intervention share).

**1. The stack (R4X, `hyp_stack_three_levers_r4`).** The predicted -0.0024 is a naive sum of three separately-measured deltas (precond -0.001147 t=-12.3, ve=1 -0.001487 t=-12.2, swdiv=4 ~-0.002 device-corrected), and the queue's own falsifier admits this ("redundant" or "antagonistic" are both live outcomes) — that's honest. But the mechanism-level worry is sharper than "redundancy": ve and swdiv move step count in *opposite* directions through the same channel. Every ve=1 cell ran fewer steps than its control (972/992, 984/1005, 993/1012 — L035), while swdiv=4 cells ran more (R2S_A_s3 1049 vs a ~1019-1020 control band). Stacked, the net step delta is not the sum of the two marginal step deltas because both interventions change per-token FLOPs and interact with the *same* packing/compute budget nonlinearly — the stack could land at a step count neither single-lever measurement predicts, and raw val_bpb (the actual verdict) is sensitive to exactly that count. Separately, L035 flags a real hazard this stack inherits doubled: ve=1 alone already dropped one cell (gpu4, the slowest device) below the ~510M-token/2-epoch boundary, turning it void. Adding swdiv=4's extra steps *helps* here (it's the one thing pushing the other direction), but precond adds zero FLOP cost and doesn't help step count at all, so the stack is not obviously safer from the boundary than ve=1 alone was — it depends on which device draws it and whether swdiv's step gain outraces ve's step cost on that specific device. If the stack underperforms the sum, you cannot cleanly separate "the three mechanisms are redundant in what they do to quality-per-token" from "one cell hit the epoch boundary and got silently truncated to final_epoch 1.0, dragging the mean down by a huge, non-mechanistic amount" — L035's own gpu4 cell moved by roughly the same order of magnitude as the entire predicted stack effect. The only way to distinguish these is to check `final_epoch` on every arm *before* trusting the mean (which the falsifier does list, credit for that), and, if a boundary cell shows up, exclude it and refit rather than average it in — otherwise "redundant" and "epoch-boundary artifact" are indistinguishable from the topline number alone.

**2. Policy changes.** (a) Exempting the axis holding the campaign best from the dry rule, later re-keyed to device-corrected effect: **principled, and the fix was necessary, not just convenient.** The comment in `direction.py` (lines ~322-340) documents the actual failure mode — a strong result on one axis (swdiv) drove every other axis dry by beating the *global* running best, which would eventually retire the whole search space on one number. The re-keying to `best_eff` (device-corrected) is the more important fix: keying on raw val_bpb let gpu7 (fastest device) manufacture a "held best" that was a device artifact, exactly the confound this campaign refuses to headline elsewhere (see L024's identical device-inflation problem in the precond significance claim). The `since_best < DRY_STREAK` guard also stops the exemption from being unbounded. This is a genuine bug fix, not a bypass — but it was discovered and patched only *after* it had already blocked something the operator wanted to run, which is a pattern worth naming: three of these four policy changes were triggered by an experiment being refused, not by proactive review. That's a process smell even when the fix itself is correct.

(b) "Reusing an axis's best-known VALUE is exploitation, exempt from the dry block": **also principled on its face** — the code comment is explicit that the alternative bug was real (ve=1 got proven a winner and then became permanently unusable because the four runs that proved it also closed the axis). But the exemption is narrower than it sounds and worth stress-testing: `_exploring()` checks `cfg.get(a) != bv` per axis independently, so R4X passes because `ve==1` matches ve's best_value and `swdiv==4` matches swdiv's best_value — each axis individually is "exploitation." Nothing in `blocked_reason` checks whether the *combination* has ever been validated, because combinations aren't an axis. This is fine as a documented design boundary (mechanism-crossed-with-closed-axis is still checked separately), but it means "exploitation" is defined per-parameter, not per-configuration, and a three-way stack that has literally never run before is being waved through a gate whose name (dry rule) suggests it's protecting against untested configurations. It isn't — E4/hypothesis registration is the only thing standing between "reusing a best value" and "running something entirely new for the first time." That's acceptable as a division of labor as long as the operator doesn't conflate "the dry rule didn't block it" with "this is low-risk."

(c) E3 skipping hypotheses named in a registered `non_activation` lesson: **principled, narrowly scoped, and the narrow scoping is what makes it defensible.** `coe.py` requires the lesson to explicitly name the hypothesis in its `evidence` field before the audit stops flagging it (lines 200-202, 245-246) — nothing is silenced without a written record explaining why, and CLAUDE.md's own rule that non-activation "may not block a direction" makes this the correct implementation of an existing policy, not a new bypass. The one thing to watch: this only excuses the *audit* from re-flagging a known-broken diagnostic; it does not retroactively fix the diagnostic or re-run the hypothesis under a working activation test (L039's qk_suppress result is a real, reproducible +0.001226 that remains scientifically unattributed — inconclusive, not negative — even after the audit stops complaining about it). If the campaign later treats "E3 clean" as "science settled" for qk_suppress, that would be the actual bypass; the code change itself is fine.

Common thread across (a)-(c): every one of these was authored and applied by the operator in the same session that needed it to unblock a specific queued experiment. Each change has a defensible written rationale and (for a, c) a documented prior failure mode it fixes. None of them is obviously wrong. But "the rule that just blocked me was wrong" being discovered exactly when it blocks something, three separate times in one session, is the kind of pattern that would be worth a retrospective check by someone who didn't write the fix — e.g., are there symmetric cases where a rule change would have made a *disliked* result launchable, and did those get evaluated with equal rigor, or only the runs pointing at a predicted win?

**3. Single seed, ve t=-12.2, 2 df.** May claim: at seed 42, on this host, at this operating point (~1000 steps, ~510M tokens, mostly-2-epoch), ve=1 produces a consistent, same-sign, same-GPU-paired quality effect roughly an order of magnitude above the measured device-counterbalanced resolution (L035's own text: t=-12.2 against resolution 0.000267, sd 0.000211 across 3 pairings — note the 4th cell, gpu4, is *void*, so this is really n=3 pairings, 2 df, not 4). May NOT claim: that the effect generalizes across initialization or data order (L028 says this explicitly and campaign-wide — every one of 54+ valid runs shares seed 42), that -0.001487 is the true magnitude rather than one draw from whatever the seed-to-seed range turns out to be, or that a t-statistic computed from n=3 same-seed pairings is a significance test in the conventional sense at all — it's a precision statement about *this seed's* device-corrected estimate, not a generalization claim. L028's own remedy — a seed-43 repeat of the precond comparison — was proposed specifically because "the campaign has been quoting precision where it has no estimate of reproducibility," and that repeat has not yet been run for ve, only flagged as needed for precond. The stack (R4X) inherits this exactly: it is being launched at seed 42 with no seed-43 companion queued, so whatever verdict it returns will carry the same unresolved reproducibility gap as precond did before L028, on a compound intervention where seed sensitivity plausibly compounds across three simultaneous mechanisms rather than being bounded by any single-lever seed check.

**4. Instrument trustworthiness.** 38 active lessons: 18 integrity, 5 overclaim, 3 scope, 3 resource, 1 runtime = 30 process/instrument failures, vs. 6 typed `valid_negative` — and even that 6 is generous, since two of them (L021, L035) are confirmed *wins*, not negatives; the type is being used as "settled non-null result," so the count of genuine scientific negatives is closer to 4 (L011 ns, L032 wd_const, L037 unet, L040 tbs-doubling). That's roughly 30:4, not the milder ratio the framing might suggest. More importantly, the error rate is not flat over time — L020, L024, L027, L028, L030, L033, L036 are all *later* corrections of *earlier* lessons or tools (L017 is literally marked RETRACTED in its own filename), meaning the instrument has been self-correcting but also that recent "confirmed" results (precond, ve) were confirmed using a device model and significance methodology that itself needed two rounds of repair mid-campaign. The honest read: the instrument is better than it was, not yet proven stable — the fact that a single-seed, cross-GPU-pairing error (L024) sat undetected long enough to produce a headlined "4.3x resolution" claim before being caught is the strongest evidence the error rate is still the binding constraint, not a solved problem. Trust it enough to keep using it; do not trust it enough to skip the sanity checks (final_epoch, GPU field, activation diagnostic separation) that caught the last five.

**5. Lessons the queued stack risks violating.** L035's own `do` field is the most direct hit: it says treat ve=1 as confirmed but explicitly NOT as a settled magnitude, single-seed, with a known epoch-boundary hazard "for any parameter-adding treatment here" — R4X is exactly that, now compounded with two more levers. L028's seed-uniformity finding applies with no mitigation queued for this specific combination. L033 (component share ≠ intervention share) is directly implicated if the stack's shortfall from -0.0024 gets narrated as "each mechanism's independent contribution was X%" without re-deriving shares from the joint run. L029 (control budget outgrew its purpose) is worth a check given R4X deliberately keeps the control unchanged rather than folding in confirmed levers — that's stated as intentional (protecting the 39-run control corpus), which is reasonable, but it does mean the control corpus keeps growing while the frontier moves, and at some point the unchanged control stops being the right question to ask. None of these block the launch outright — they bound what the result may claim once it lands.
